How to choose a personal trainer

Jessica Fazzari Coaching · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

To choose a good personal trainer, check that they hold a Certificate IV in Fitness and current first aid, carry public liability and professional indemnity insurance, and have experience with goals like yours. Then look at what the price actually includes and whether you feel comfortable with how they communicate. A short chat before you commit is the easiest way to find out.

Check their credentials

1. Check their qualifications

In Australia, the Certificate IV in Fitness is the standard qualification for personal trainers. The Certificate III qualifies someone as a gym or group fitness instructor. The Better Health Channel recommends checking a trainer's qualifications before you start.

Do not be shy about asking. A qualified trainer will be glad to tell you what they hold. For more detail, see personal trainer qualifications in Australia.

2. Make sure first aid and CPR are current

The Better Health Channel also suggests checking that your trainer has current first aid training. CPR certificates need regular renewal, so ask whether theirs is up to date.

3. Ask about insurance

A trainer should hold public liability and professional indemnity insurance, according to the Better Health Channel. It protects both of you if something goes wrong. If a trainer works inside a gym, it is still worth asking whether they have their own cover.

Check they are the right fit for you

4. Look for experience with goals like yours

Every trainer has areas they enjoy and know well. Someone who mostly works with endurance athletes may not be the best fit if you want to learn to lift, and vice versa. Ask:

  • Have you worked with people with similar goals to mine?
  • How would you approach my first few months?
  • What does progress usually look like for clients like me?

Be wary of anyone who promises a specific result by a specific date. Good trainers talk about process and realistic timelines. See how long it takes to see results.

5. Ask for references or reviews

The Better Health Channel also suggests asking for references. Reviews, word of mouth and a trainer's social media can give you a feel for how they work and who they work with. Just remember that a polished Instagram is not the same as good coaching.

6. Understand what the price includes

Two trainers with the same hourly rate can offer very different value. Before you sign up, find out:

  • How long sessions are.
  • Whether you get a written program for the days you train on your own.
  • Whether check-ins or messaging between sessions are included.
  • Whether you need to pay for gym entry separately.
  • Whether there are pack discounts, and how long you have to use them.

For typical prices, read how much a personal trainer costs in Australia.

7. Pay attention to communication and comfort

You will spend a lot of time with your trainer, so it matters that you feel comfortable with them. Notice whether they:

  • Listen to you rather than talking over you.
  • Explain why you are doing each exercise.
  • Respect your limits, injuries and preferences.
  • Make you feel welcome in the gym, whatever your starting point.
  • Reply to messages in a reasonable time.

A trainer who makes you feel judged or rushed is unlikely to keep you coming back, no matter how qualified they are.

8. Think about location and format

Practical details make a big difference to whether you stick with it. Is the gym close to home or work? Do their available times suit you? Would you be better off with in-person sessions, online coaching, or a mix of both? Our guide to online coaching vs personal training can help you decide.

A good trainer will ask about your health history at the start. If you have an injury or a medical condition, check with your GP or physio before starting, and choose a trainer who is happy to work within their advice.

Use a first chat to decide

Most trainers offer an initial chat or consult before you commit. Use it well. Come with your goals, your available days and any injuries written down, and ask the questions above. Pay attention to how much the trainer asks about you compared with how much they talk about themselves.

After the chat, ask yourself:

  • Did they listen and understand what I want?
  • Did they explain how they would approach my training in a way that made sense?
  • Was the price clear, with no surprises?
  • Do I feel comfortable training with this person?

If the answer to all of these is yes, you have probably found a good fit. If not, it is fine to keep looking. The right trainer is worth the extra effort.

Red flags to watch for

  • Guaranteed results or quick-fix promises.
  • No questions about your health, injuries or history.
  • The same generic program for every client.
  • Pressure to buy large packs before you have trained together.
  • Advice well outside their scope, such as diagnosing injuries or prescribing supplements.

Quick answers

What should I ask a personal trainer before hiring them?

Ask about their qualifications, first aid, insurance, experience with goals like yours, what the price includes and how they keep in touch between sessions.

What insurance should a personal trainer have?

The Better Health Channel recommends checking that a trainer has public liability and professional indemnity insurance.

What are red flags in a personal trainer?

Guaranteed results, no questions about your health history, generic programs for everyone, pressure to buy big packs, and advice outside their scope are all warning signs.

Does it matter if I like my personal trainer?

Yes. Feeling comfortable with your trainer makes it much more likely you will keep turning up, which is what drives results over time.
