Does online coaching work, and is it worth it?

Jessica Fazzari Coaching · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

Online coaching can work very well – for the right person, with the right program. It tends to suit people who are fairly self-motivated, have access to a gym and are happy to follow a plan between check-ins. It works best when the program is genuinely personalised and includes regular check-ins, video form feedback and a way to message your coach.

How online coaching works

With online coaching, your trainer isn't standing next to you. Instead, you typically get:

  • A training program written for your goals, schedule and equipment.
  • An app with your workouts, exercise demonstration videos and a place to log your sets.
  • Regular check-ins where you report on training, energy, sleep and progress.
  • Feedback on your technique from videos you film of your lifts.
  • Messaging support for questions between check-ins.
  • Program adjustments as you progress.

You do the sessions on your own, at times that suit you. Your coach does the thinking, planning and adjusting in the background.

Is online coaching effective? A balanced view

It's worth being upfront: most of the bold claims you'll see about online coaching's effectiveness come from businesses that sell it. That doesn't mean it doesn't work – it just means you should judge a program on what it actually includes, not the marketing.

The fundamentals of getting stronger and fitter are the same whether you're coached online or in person: a sensible program, progressive overload, good technique, consistency and enough recovery. Online coaching can deliver most of those well. What it can't fully replace is someone physically there to adjust your position mid-set or help you set up unfamiliar equipment.

So the honest answer is: online coaching works when the person following it actually trains consistently, and the coach provides real, individual feedback. For a side-by-side comparison, see online coaching vs personal training.

Who online coaching suits best

Online coaching is often a great fit if you:

  • Are reasonably self-motivated and will do your sessions without someone waiting for you.
  • Have gym access – or a well-equipped home set-up that your program can be built around.
  • Know the basics of moving around a gym, or are willing to learn through videos and feedback.
  • Have a changing schedule and need to train at different times each week.
  • Live away from a trainer you want to work with, or travel often.
  • Want coaching on a smaller budget than regular in-person sessions.

Who might be better with in-person training

Online coaching may not be the best starting point if you:

  • Are completely new to the gym and feel unsure about equipment or technique.
  • Find it very hard to stay consistent without a booked appointment.
  • Are coming back from an injury and need closer supervision (check with your GP or physio first).
  • Simply enjoy and are motivated by having a coach in the room.

A hybrid approach – some in-person sessions plus an online program – can bridge the gap. If you're new to training, our guide to personal training for beginners may help.

What a good online coaching program includes

Use this checklist when comparing coaches:

  1. An individual plan. Your program should reflect your goals, experience, schedule and equipment – not a generic template with your name on it.
  2. Regular check-ins. Weekly or fortnightly check-ins where your coach reviews how things are going and responds.
  3. Video form checks. A way to send videos of your lifts and get specific technique feedback.
  4. Messaging access. The ability to ask questions between check-ins, with a clear idea of when you'll hear back.
  5. Program adjustments. Changes based on your progress, not the same plan repeated.
  6. A qualified coach. Check their qualifications, just as you would for an in-person trainer.

Red flags to watch out for

  • Generic templates sold as "personalised".
  • No check-ins, or check-ins that get no real response.
  • No way to get form feedback.
  • Big promises about how much weight you'll lose or how fast you'll change.
  • Individualised meal plans or supplement prescriptions from someone without the qualifications for it (see can personal trainers give nutrition advice).
  • Unclear pricing about what's actually included.
The best program is one you'll actually follow. Before you sign up, ask a coach exactly how often they'll check in, how quickly they reply and how they'll adjust your plan.

Is online coaching worth the money?

Online coaching usually costs less than seeing a trainer in person several times a week, because the coach isn't spending an hour with you each session. Whether it's worth it comes down to two things: does the program include genuine individual coaching, and will you use it? If both are true, it can be very good value. Quick answers

Is online coaching as good as a personal trainer?

It can be for people who are self-motivated and comfortable in the gym. It can't fully replace hands-on cueing, so complete beginners often benefit from some in-person sessions.

Can beginners do online coaching?

Yes, if they're willing to learn from exercise videos and send form-check videos for feedback. Some beginners prefer to start in person and move online later.

How often should an online coach check in?

Weekly or fortnightly check-ins are common. What matters most is that your coach actually reviews your progress and adjusts your plan.

Do I need a gym for online coaching?

Not always, but gym access gives your coach more options. If you train at home, make sure your coach builds the program around the equipment you have.
