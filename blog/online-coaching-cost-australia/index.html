How much does online personal training cost in Australia?

Jessica Fazzari Coaching · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

Online personal training in Australia ranges widely – from roughly $7 a week for basic app-based programs to $50 or more a week for coaching with regular check-ins, according to Superprof. The more personalised and hands-on the coaching, the more it tends to cost. Those figures come from providers, so treat them as a rough guide rather than an exact market rate.

Typical online coaching prices in Australia

There's no official price list for online coaching, and published figures mostly come from businesses in the industry. With that caveat, Superprof puts app and check-in style programs at around $7 to $50+ per week. Higher-touch coaching, with more frequent contact and detailed feedback, sits at the upper end or above.

A useful way to think about it is in tiers:

Type of online coachingWhat you usually getWhere it sits on price
Template or app-only programPre-written workouts, little or no contact with a coachLower end
Personalised program with periodic check-insProgram written for you, check-ins every week or two, some adjustmentsMiddle
High-touch coachingWeekly check-ins, messaging, video form feedback, ongoing changes, nutrition guidanceUpper end

Because the range is so wide, comparing prices on their own isn't very helpful. What matters is what's included.

What drives the price of online coaching

Custom program vs template

A program written for your goals, experience and equipment takes a coach real time to build and update. A template takes none after it's written. That's the single biggest difference between cheap and more expensive options.

Check-in frequency

Weekly check-ins cost more than fortnightly or monthly ones, because each one involves a coach reviewing your data and responding. Video check-ins tend to take longer again.

Form feedback

Reviewing your lift videos and giving specific technique cues is time-intensive, so programs that include it usually cost more.

Messaging access

Being able to message your coach between check-ins adds value – and coach time.

Nutrition and habit support

General nutrition education, recipe ideas and accountability support are often included in higher tiers. Keep in mind that individualised meal plans sit outside a standard PT's scope; see can personal trainers give nutrition advice.

Coach experience and demand

As with in-person training, more experienced or in-demand coaches may charge more.

Online coaching vs in-person PT: cost comparison

In-person sessions cost more per hour of coaching because the trainer is with you the whole time. Nationally, Bark (January 2026) puts a session at around $40–$90, with an average of about $75. If you trained with a PT two or three times a week, that adds up quickly.

Online coaching spreads the coach's time across your whole week – programming, check-ins and feedback – for a weekly fee that's usually lower than a single in-person session. The trade-off is that you don't get someone physically there to cue you during each set. For the full picture on in-person prices, see how much a personal trainer costs in Australia.

Other costs to factor in

The coaching fee usually isn't the only cost. When you're budgeting, also think about:

  • Gym access. Most online programs assume you can train in a gym, so a membership is often a separate cost.
  • Home equipment. If you train at home, you may need some basic gear for your program to progress over time.
  • Food. Supporting your training with regular, balanced meals may change your grocery shop a little.
  • Optional in-person sessions. Some people add the occasional face-to-face session to check technique on the main lifts.

Looking at the total cost makes it easier to compare online coaching fairly against other options.

What you should get for your money

Whatever you pay, check that the program includes:

  • A training plan written for you, not just a generic template.
  • An app or platform with exercise demonstrations and somewhere to log your workouts.
  • Regular, scheduled check-ins – and a clear idea of how often.
  • A way to get feedback on your technique.
  • Program adjustments as you progress.
  • A coach with recognised fitness qualifications.

And ask about anything that's not clear: how fast messages are answered, what happens if you miss a week, and how the program changes over time.

Cheapest isn't always best value. A $10 template you stop using after two weeks costs more per result than a coached program you stick with. Choose the level of support you'll actually use.

Is online coaching worth it?

For people who are fairly self-motivated and have gym access, online coaching can be a cost-effective way to get expert programming and accountability. If you're brand new to the gym, a few in-person sessions to learn the basics can be worth adding. Read more in does online coaching work.

Quick answers

Is online personal training cheaper than in-person?

Usually, yes. A week of online coaching often costs less than a single in-person session, because the coach isn't with you for every workout.

Why are some online programs so cheap?

Low-cost programs are often templates with little or no contact with a coach. More personalised programs with regular check-ins and form feedback cost more because they take more coach time.

How is online coaching usually charged?

Many coaches charge weekly or monthly. Check exactly what's included, such as how often you'll check in and whether you get form feedback.

Can I claim online coaching on private health insurance?

It depends on your fund, your level of extras cover and the provider. Check directly with your health fund before assuming you can claim.
