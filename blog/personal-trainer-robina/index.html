How personal trainers in Robina usually charge

Most personal trainers charge per session. On the Gold Coast, trainers listed on Bark charge roughly $65–$110 per session, while typical Queensland prices sit around $50–$80. Nationally, Bark puts the range at roughly $40–$90 per session with an average around $75 as of January 2026 (Bark).

Some trainers offer session packs. Bark notes that packs of 10 or more sessions are often around 10–20% cheaper per session. That helps, but you're still paying for time with your trainer, and the days you train alone may or may not be covered.

Why the days between sessions matter

Australia's physical activity guidelines recommend that adults aged 18–64 do muscle-strengthening activity on at least two days a week, plus 150–300 minutes of moderate activity (or 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity) each week. Unless you're seeing a trainer most days, most of that happens on your own.

That's where a lot of progress is won or lost. Without a plan, it's easy to repeat the same workout for months, guess at weights, or skip sessions when life gets busy. Having a written program, logging what you lift and checking in with someone who adjusts the plan keeps the whole week moving in one direction.

When per-session training is the better choice

Weekly coaching isn't automatically the right answer. Paying per session can make more sense if:

  • you only want an occasional session to check your technique or refresh your program
  • you already have a program you're happy with and just want supervision on certain lifts
  • you're experienced, self-motivated and don't need check-ins between sessions

On the other hand, weekly coaching is usually better value if you'd otherwise book two or more sessions a week, you want help on the days you train alone, or accountability is what you've been missing. Ask yourself honestly where you tend to fall off: in the gym with a trainer, or on the days in between.

What to look for in a Robina personal trainer

Price is only one part of the decision. The Better Health Channel recommends checking qualifications including first aid, experience, references, and public liability and professional indemnity insurance. In Australia, the Certificate IV in Fitness is the standard qualification for personal trainers. It's also worth asking what's included in the price and how your trainer will communicate with you.
