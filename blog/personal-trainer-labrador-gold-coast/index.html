Finding a personal trainer on the northern Gold Coast

The northern suburbs around the Broadwater, from Labrador up through Biggera Waters and Runaway Bay, are well served by gyms. Finding a coach who suits you is a different question. Most searches for a personal trainer in Labrador lead to directories with long lists of names and not much detail on price, location or what you actually get.

A more useful approach is to decide what kind of coaching you need first, then find the person who offers it. For most people, it comes down to three choices:

  • Fully in person: you train with your coach each session. The most hands-on option, and usually the most expensive.
  • Fully online: your coach writes your program and checks in remotely, and you train on your own at a gym near you.
  • Hybrid: a mix, typically one in-person session a week plus an online program for the rest.

Five questions to ask a Labrador personal trainer before you book

  1. What are your qualifications? The Certificate IV in Fitness is the standard qualification for personal trainers in Australia. Registration bodies such as AUSactive also look for current first aid and CPR. See personal trainer qualifications in Australia for more.
  2. Are you insured? The Better Health Channel suggests checking that a trainer holds public liability and professional indemnity insurance, as well as asking about their experience and references.
  3. What does the price include? Ask whether you get a written program for the days you train alone, how often you'll check in, and whether you can get feedback on your technique between sessions.
  4. Where and when do you train clients? A trainer on the other side of the city at a time you can't make isn't going to work, however good they are.
  5. How do you track progress? Look for someone who measures more than the scales, such as strength, energy, how your clothes fit, or measurements if you want them.

Fitting training into a northern suburbs week

The biggest predictor of progress is usually consistency, not the perfect program. A few practical ideas for building training into your week:

  • Pick your non-negotiable days. Australia's physical activity guidelines recommend muscle-strengthening activity on at least two days a week, alongside 150–300 minutes of moderate activity (or 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity) across the week. Start by locking in two strength days.
  • Train close to home on your own days. If you're on a hybrid or online program, you can do your independent sessions at whichever gym is easiest to get to from Labrador or Runaway Bay.
  • Count the walking. Walks along the Broadwater all count towards your weekly moderate activity.
  • Plan for busy weeks. A shorter session done is better than a perfect one skipped. A good coach can give you a cut-down version of your program for weeks that get away from you.
