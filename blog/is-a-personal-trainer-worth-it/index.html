Is a personal trainer worth it?

Jessica Fazzari Coaching · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

For most people starting out, coming back after time off, or stuck on a plateau, a personal trainer is worth it. Research has found that people supervised by qualified trainers made bigger gains than people training on their own, and a trainer also gives you structure, technique coaching and accountability. If you are already experienced and self-motivated, you may not need one, or a cheaper option like online coaching may be enough.

What the research says about personal trainers

A 2014 randomised controlled trial compared people who trained under the supervision of qualified personal trainers with people who followed a self-directed program. The supervised group gained more lean mass and improved their fitness more than the group training on their own.

That does not mean a trainer does the work for you. It suggests that having someone plan your training, push the intensity to the right level and correct your technique helps you get more out of the same time in the gym.

The real benefits of a personal trainer

When people ask whether a personal trainer is worth the money, they are really asking what they get that they could not get alone. The main things are:

Accountability

Knowing someone is expecting you makes it much harder to skip a session. For many people this is the biggest benefit. Consistency over months matters far more than any single perfect workout.

Technique

A trainer watches how you move and coaches you to lift with good form. That helps you train the right muscles, lift heavier over time and lowers the chance of picking up a niggle from poor technique.

A plan that progresses

Random workouts feel productive but often go nowhere. A trainer writes a program that builds week to week, so you are steadily asking a bit more of your body. Jess sums it up as: "Intention only gets you so far without direction."

Working around injuries and limits

A good trainer asks about your history and adjusts exercises to suit you. If you have an injury or a medical condition, check with your GP or physio first, and let your trainer know so they can work within that advice.

Who gets the most out of a personal trainer?

A personal trainer tends to be worth it most for:

  • Beginners who do not know where to start and want to learn the basics safely. See personal training for beginners.
  • People who have hit a plateau and are doing the same things without progress.
  • People returning to training after an injury, a busy period, pregnancy or time away, who want to rebuild sensibly.
  • Anyone who struggles with consistency and knows they train better when someone is checking in.

When a personal trainer might not be worth it

To be honest about it, not everyone needs a trainer. If you have been lifting for years, you understand how to progress a program, your technique is solid and you rarely miss a session, you may be able to get great results on your own.

A trainer is also less useful if you cannot commit to the program between sessions. One session a week helps, but most of your progress comes from the training you do on your own days, along with sleep, food and daily habits.

And if the cost would stretch your budget to the point of stress, it is better to choose a cheaper option you can keep up with than an expensive one you stop after a month.

Cheaper ways to get the benefits

You can still get structure, technique feedback and accountability without paying for several in-person sessions a week:

  • Fewer sessions. See a trainer weekly or fortnightly and follow their program in between. Read how often you should see a personal trainer.
  • Online coaching. A personalised program with check-ins and video form feedback, usually for less than the cost of one session a week. Our comparison of online coaching vs personal training explains the trade-offs.
  • Hybrid coaching. Online coaching plus a regular in-person session, so you get hands-on coaching without paying for every workout.

For a breakdown of typical prices, see how much a personal trainer costs in Australia.

How to make a personal trainer worth the money

If you do decide to work with a trainer, you can get a lot more value by:

  • Being clear and honest about your goals, schedule and history from the start.
  • Doing the program on your own days, not just turning up to sessions.
  • Asking questions so you understand why you are doing each exercise.
  • Tracking more than the scales: strength, energy, sleep and how your clothes fit.
  • Giving it a fair amount of time before judging the results.

Quick answers

Do personal trainers actually make a difference?

Research suggests they can. A 2014 randomised controlled trial found people supervised by qualified trainers gained more lean mass and fitness than people following a self-directed program.

Is a personal trainer worth it for beginners?

Usually, yes. Beginners benefit from learning safe technique, having a clear plan and building the confidence to train on their own.

Can I get the benefits of a personal trainer for less money?

Yes. Seeing a trainer less often, online coaching, or a hybrid of online coaching and occasional in-person sessions can give you structure and feedback for a lower weekly cost.

When is a personal trainer not worth it?

If you are an experienced, self-motivated lifter who knows how to progress a program, or if the cost would stop you training consistently, you may be better off on your own or with a cheaper option.
