How often should I see a personal trainer?

Jessica Fazzari Coaching · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

Beginners commonly see a personal trainer two to three times a week for the first six weeks or so, then drop back to once or twice a week as they get confident. Experienced lifters often check in weekly, fortnightly or even monthly. The right frequency is the one you can keep up, as long as you are following a program on the days you train on your own.

How often should beginners see a personal trainer?

When you are new to the gym, there is a lot to learn: how to set up equipment, how each exercise should feel, how heavy to go and how to warm up. Seeing a trainer more often at the start speeds that up.

A common approach, described by Crunch Fitness, is two to three sessions a week for around the first six weeks, then tapering to one or two sessions a week. This is a general guide rather than a rule, but it reflects how most people learn: lots of coaching early, then more independence.

If you are just starting out, our guide to personal training for beginners covers what to focus on in those first weeks.

How often for experienced gym-goers?

Once you know your way around the gym and can train safely on your own, you need less hands-on time. Many experienced people see a trainer:

  • Weekly, to keep technique sharp on the main lifts and stay accountable.
  • Fortnightly, to review the program and make adjustments.
  • Monthly, mainly for a fresh program and a check on progress.

At this stage, the value is less about learning exercises and more about having someone plan your progression, spot problems early and keep you honest.

Consistency matters more than frequency

It is tempting to think more sessions means faster results. In reality, what drives progress is training consistently over months, not how many of those sessions have a trainer standing next to you.

Three sessions a week for a month and then nothing will do less for you than one session a week for six months. When you choose a frequency, ask yourself:

  • Can I afford this every week without it becoming stressful?
  • Does it fit around work, family and everything else?
  • Will I still be doing this in three months?

If the honest answer is no, pick a smaller commitment you can stick to.

What to do between sessions

Most people who see a trainer once or twice a week are also training on their own two or three other days. Those solo sessions are where a lot of the progress happens, so they need structure too.

A good trainer will give you a written program for your own days, explain how to progress it and check in on how it is going. Look for:

  • A clear plan with exercises, sets, reps and how to add load over time.
  • Exercise videos or notes so you remember how to do each movement.
  • A way to track your workouts so your trainer can see what you did.
  • Some way to ask questions or get form feedback between sessions.

For general activity targets beyond your gym sessions, see how much exercise you need each week.

If you are coming back from an injury or managing a medical condition, check with your GP or physio about how much training is right for you, and let your trainer know.

The hybrid approach: one session plus an online program

A middle ground that works well for many people is one in-person session a week, combined with an online program for the rest of the week. You get regular hands-on coaching on technique, and the online side covers your solo days with a structured plan and check-ins.

This keeps the cost lower than seeing a trainer three times a week, while giving you more support than training alone. If you are weighing up the options, our post on online coaching vs personal training goes through who suits each.

How to tell if you need more or fewer sessions

Your ideal frequency can change over time. Signs you might benefit from more sessions:

  • You are unsure how to do your program on your own days.
  • Your technique slips when nobody is watching.
  • You keep skipping solo sessions.
  • You are learning new, more technical lifts.

Signs you could drop back:

  • You are confident doing your program on your own.
  • You are training consistently without reminders.
  • Sessions mostly feel like someone watching you do what you already know.

It is also worth reviewing every month or two. For more on planning the bigger picture, see how many personal training sessions you need.

Quick answers

Is once a week with a personal trainer enough?

It can be, as long as you also follow a program on your own days. One session a week is enough for many people to keep technique on track and stay accountable.

How many times a week should a beginner see a personal trainer?

A common approach is two to three sessions a week for around the first six weeks, then tapering to one or two as you get more confident.

Should I train on days I do not see my trainer?

Yes, if you can. Most progress comes from training consistently across the week, so ask your trainer for a program to follow on your own days.

Can I see a personal trainer once a month?

Experienced lifters sometimes do, mainly to get a new program and a progress check. It works best if you are confident training safely on your own.
