How much does a personal trainer cost in Australia?

Jessica Fazzari Coaching · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

Most personal trainers in Australia charge roughly $40 to $100 for a one-on-one session, with many sitting somewhere around $50 to $80. The exact price depends on the trainer's experience, where you live, how long the session runs and whether you buy sessions in a pack. Online coaching is usually cheaper again, because you pay weekly for a program and check-ins rather than per hour.

Average personal trainer prices in Australia

There is no single set rate for personal training, but the published price guides land in a fairly similar range. These are approximate and change over time, so treat them as a starting point rather than a quote.

SourceTypical price per session
Bark (January 2026)About $40 to $90, average around $75

In short, if you are quoted something in the $50 to $90 range for a standard one-on-one session, that is normal for Australia. Prices well under that are often for shorter sessions, group sessions or newer trainers. Prices well above it usually reflect a very experienced trainer, a premium location, or a session that includes extras.

What affects the cost of a personal trainer?

Two trainers in the same suburb can charge quite different rates. The main things that push the price up or down are:

  • Experience and qualifications. A trainer who has been coaching for years, or who has extra study in a particular area, will usually charge more than someone who has just finished their Cert IV.
  • Location. Capital cities and busy areas tend to cost more than regional towns. If you are on the Gold Coast, see our guide to personal trainer prices on the Gold Coast.
  • Session length. A 30-minute session costs less than a 60-minute one, though usually not half the price. Read more in how long a personal training session is.
  • One-on-one or group. Small-group or partner training splits the trainer's time, so each person pays less.
  • Where you train. Some trainers work inside a gym, some come to you, and some train people outdoors. Mobile trainers sometimes charge more to cover travel.

Are session packs cheaper?

Often, yes. Many trainers offer a lower rate per session if you buy a block up front. According to Bark, packs of 10 or more sessions are often around 10 to 20 per cent cheaper per session than paying as you go.

A pack can be good value if you know you will use it. Before you buy one, it is worth asking a few simple questions:

  • How long do I have to use the sessions?
  • What happens if I am sick or away?
  • Does the price include a program for the days I train on my own?
  • Is there anything extra, such as gym entry, that I pay separately?

What do you actually get for the money?

It is easy to compare personal trainers on price alone, but the hour in the gym is only part of what you are paying for. A good trainer should be giving you:

  • An assessment of where you are starting from, including any injuries or limits.
  • A program that progresses over time, rather than random workouts.
  • Coaching on technique so you lift safely and get more from each exercise.
  • Accountability, so you keep showing up when motivation dips.

A cheaper trainer who gives you all of that can be better value than an expensive one who just counts reps. If you are weighing it up, our post on whether a personal trainer is worth it goes into this in more detail.

Cheaper ways to get personal training

If two or three sessions a week does not fit your budget, you still have options:

  • Fewer sessions plus a program. See a trainer once a week or once a fortnight and follow a written program on your other days.
  • Online coaching. You get a personalised program, check-ins and feedback through an app, usually for a weekly fee that is less than one in-person session.
  • Hybrid coaching. A mix of online coaching with a regular in-person session, so you still get hands-on help with technique.
If you have an injury or a medical condition, check with your GP or physio before starting a new training program, whichever option you choose.

How to decide what to spend

The right budget is the one you can keep up for long enough to see results. A few months of steady, affordable coaching will usually do more for you than three expensive sessions and then nothing. As Jess puts it: "Intention only gets you so far without direction."

If you are unsure which option fits, start by being clear on your goal, how many days a week you can train, and whether you want someone with you in the gym or are happy following a program on your own. Then book a free consult (details below) and talk it through before you commit to anything.

Quick answers

How much is a personal trainer per hour in Australia?

Price guides put most one-on-one sessions at roughly $50 to $100 per hour in 2026, with many trainers charging somewhere around $50 to $80. Rates vary with experience and location.

Is it cheaper to buy personal training sessions in a pack?

Usually. Bark reports that packs of 10 or more sessions are often around 10 to 20 per cent cheaper per session. Check how long you have to use them before you buy.

Is online coaching cheaper than a personal trainer?

Generally, yes. Online coaching is usually charged weekly and often costs less than one in-person session, because you train on your own with a program and check-ins rather than having the trainer with you.

Why do some personal trainers charge more than others?

Experience, location, session length, whether training is one-on-one or in a group, and whether the trainer travels to you all affect the price.
