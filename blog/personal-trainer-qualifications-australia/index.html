What qualifications does a personal trainer need in Australia?

Jessica Fazzari Coaching · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

In Australia, the Certificate IV in Fitness (SIS40221) is the standard qualification for working as a personal trainer, while the Certificate III in Fitness (SIS30321) is the qualification for a gym or group fitness instructor. Trainers registered with an industry body such as AUSactive also need current first aid and CPR. It is worth checking these before you book.

Cert III vs Cert IV in Fitness

Certificate III in Fitness (SIS30321)

The Certificate III in Fitness is the entry-level qualification for the fitness industry. It qualifies people to work as a gym instructor or group fitness instructor. Typical skills include:

  • Running gym inductions and showing people how to use equipment safely.
  • Delivering general fitness programs and group classes.
  • Pre-exercise health screening.
  • Basic anatomy and exercise principles.

Someone with only a Cert III can do valuable work, but in Australia the Cert III on its own is generally not considered a personal training qualification.

Certificate IV in Fitness (SIS40221)

The Certificate IV in Fitness is the recognised standard for personal trainers. It builds on the Cert III and covers the skills needed to work one-on-one with clients, such as:

  • Assessing clients and setting goals.
  • Designing and progressing individual programs.
  • Coaching technique for strength and conditioning.
  • Giving general healthy-eating information in line with the Australian Dietary Guidelines.
  • Knowing when to refer clients to other health professionals.

The Cert IV has limits on what it covers. For example, it does not qualify trainers to write individualised diet plans. Read more in can personal trainers give nutrition advice.

First aid, CPR and registration

First aid and CPR

A trainer working with the public should hold current first aid and CPR certificates. Industry registration requires them; AUSactive's registration requirements list current first aid and CPR alongside the relevant fitness qualification. CPR in particular needs renewing regularly, so it is reasonable to ask whether your trainer's certificate is up to date.

Registration: AUSactive and Physical Activity Australia

In Australia, personal trainers can register with industry bodies such as AUSactive and Physical Activity Australia. Registration generally shows that the trainer holds a recognised qualification, has current first aid and CPR, and keeps up their professional development.

Whether registration is a legal requirement is not clear-cut, and it should not be assumed that every trainer must be registered by law. It is still a useful signal, and some private health funds require an AUSactive-recognised provider before they will pay a rebate. If that matters to you, see claiming personal training on private health insurance.

Other things worth checking

Qualifications are the minimum. When you are comparing trainers, it also helps to look at:

  • Insurance: public liability and professional indemnity cover.
  • Experience: how long they have been training people, and whether they have worked with people with similar goals to yours.
  • Further study: extra courses in areas relevant to you.
  • Communication: whether they explain things clearly and listen to you.

How to check a trainer's qualifications

It is completely reasonable to ask a trainer about their qualifications. A good trainer will be happy to tell you. You can:

  • Ask which certificates they hold and where they studied.
  • Ask whether their first aid and CPR are current.
  • Ask whether they are registered with an industry body.
  • Check whether the gym they work at has its own requirements for trainers.
A qualified trainer will also know their limits. If you have an injury or a medical condition, they should ask you to check with your GP or physio and may refer you to another health professional.

What a qualified trainer should and should not do

Knowing what the qualifications cover also helps you understand what to expect from your trainer. A qualified personal trainer should:

  • Screen your health before you start training.
  • Assess how you move and set goals with you.
  • Write and progress a program suited to you.
  • Coach your technique and keep you accountable.
  • Give general healthy-eating information.

A personal trainer should not:

  • Diagnose or treat injuries. That is a job for a GP or physio.
  • Write individualised diet plans or prescribe supplements.
  • Advise you to ignore your doctor or stop medication.

A trainer who knows these boundaries and refers you on when needed is a good sign. It shows they put your health first and are confident enough to say when something is outside their area. The best results often come from a trainer working alongside your GP, physio or dietitian rather than trying to do everything themselves.

What qualifications don't tell you

A certificate tells you a trainer has met the minimum standard. It does not tell you whether you will enjoy working with them, whether they will keep your program progressing, or whether they will hold you accountable. Those come down to the person, so it is worth having a chat before you commit. If you are weighing up whether a trainer is worth it at all, see is a personal trainer worth it.
