How much is a personal trainer on the Gold Coast?

Jessica Fazzari Coaching · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

On the Gold Coast, a one-on-one personal training session usually costs somewhere between about $50 and $110, depending on the trainer, the session length and where you train. Queensland trainers typically sit around $50 to $80 a session, while Gold Coast listings on Bark show a range of roughly $65 to $110. Shorter sessions, packs and online coaching can all bring the cost down.

Why Gold Coast PT prices vary so much

The Gold Coast has a lot of trainers, from newly qualified PTs building a client list to coaches who have been working for many years. That spread shows up in the price. The main factors are:

  • Experience. More experienced trainers usually charge more.
  • Suburb and gym. Training in a premium gym or a busy beachside area can cost more than a local gym further from the coast.
  • One-on-one or small group. Sharing a session with a partner or small group lowers the cost per person.
  • What is included. Some trainers include a program for your solo days, check-ins or messaging. Others charge only for the time in the session.

30-minute vs 60-minute sessions

Not every session is an hour. Many Gold Coast trainers offer 30, 45 and 60 minute options.

  • 30 minutes suits people who are short on time, already know the basics, or want a focused technique session. It costs less, but you will not fit as much in.
  • 45 minutes is enough for a proper warm-up, the main lifts and some accessory work, without much downtime.
  • 60 minutes gives more room for longer warm-ups, mobility work or a bigger session, and is common for first sessions.

A shorter session is usually not half the price of an hour, so it is worth comparing what you get per minute as well as the total. Our post on how long a personal training session is covers this in more detail.

In-gym vs mobile personal training

On the Gold Coast you will find trainers who work inside a gym and trainers who come to you, whether that is your home, a park or the beach.

In-gym training gives you access to racks, barbells, dumbbells and machines, which makes it easier to progress strength training over time. You may need a gym membership on top of the trainer's fee, so check that when you compare prices.

Mobile training is convenient, but trainers often charge more to cover travel, and you are limited to the equipment they can bring. It can work well for general fitness, but it is harder to keep adding load for strength goals.

Ways to keep the cost down

You do not have to see a trainer three times a week to get good coaching. Some options that are easier on the budget:

  • Buy a pack if your trainer offers a lower rate for a block of sessions, and make sure you know how long you have to use them.
  • See a trainer less often and follow a written program on your own days. Read how often you should see a personal trainer for a guide.
  • Try online coaching, where you train on your own with a personalised program and regular check-ins. See online personal training on the Gold Coast.
  • Mix the two with a hybrid package that combines online coaching with a regular in-person session.
Whatever you choose, if you have an injury or a medical condition, check with your GP or physio before you start a new program.

Getting value from your trainer

The cheapest trainer is not always the best value, and the most expensive is not always the best coach. Look for someone who assesses where you are starting, writes a program that progresses, coaches your technique and keeps you accountable. That is what turns good intentions into results, or as Jess says, "Train with purpose."

Quick answers

How much does a personal trainer cost per session on the Gold Coast?

Gold Coast listings on Bark show roughly $65 to $110 per session, with Queensland trainers more generally charging around $50 to $80. Prices change with experience, session length and location.

Do I need a gym membership as well as a personal trainer?

If your trainer works inside a gym, you will usually need access to that gym, so check whether entry is included. Mobile trainers who come to you do not need a gym, but often charge more for travel.

Is a 30-minute personal training session worth it?

It can be, especially if you are short on time or already know the basics. You will fit less in than a 45 or 60 minute session, and it is usually not half the price of an hour.
