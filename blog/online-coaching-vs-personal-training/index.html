Online coaching vs in-person personal training: which is better?

Jessica Fazzari Coaching · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

Neither is better for everyone. In-person personal training gives you hands-on technique coaching and someone in the room keeping you going, while online coaching is cheaper, more flexible and works wherever you train. Many people get the best of both with a hybrid: an online program for most of the week plus a regular in-person session.

What is the difference between online coaching and personal training?

In-person personal training means your trainer is with you for the session. They set up equipment, demonstrate exercises, watch every rep and adjust things on the spot.

Online coaching means you follow a personalised program on your own, usually through an app, and your coach supports you remotely. That typically includes check-ins, messaging, and reviewing videos of your lifts so they can give feedback on your form.

Both should be built around you, your goals and your schedule. The difference is how much of the coaching happens live.

Pros and cons of each option

The pros of in-person personal training

  • Hands-on cueing. A trainer can see and correct your technique instantly, which is especially useful for new or technical lifts.
  • Equipment set-up. They show you how to set up racks, benches and machines so you feel comfortable in the gym.
  • In-room motivation. Having someone there pushes many people to work harder and makes skipping a session less likely.
  • Immediate adjustments. If something feels wrong, your trainer can swap the exercise straight away.

The main downsides are cost and flexibility. You pay for the trainer's time, and you need to fit your sessions around their availability and location. See how much a personal trainer costs in Australia for typical prices.

The pros of online coaching

  • Lower cost. You pay weekly for programming and support, which usually costs less than a single in-person session.
  • Flexibility. You train when it suits you, whether that is 5am or 9pm.
  • Train anywhere. Your coach does not need to be in the same suburb, or even the same state.
  • Video form checks. You film your lifts and your coach gives feedback, so you still get technique coaching.
  • More frequent contact. Messaging and regular check-ins can mean more touchpoints across the week than a once-a-week session.

The trade-off is that nobody is standing next to you. You need to be reasonably self-motivated, and you need access to a gym or suitable equipment. For more on this, read does online coaching work.

Who suits online coaching vs in-person training?

Who suits in-person personal training?

In-person training tends to suit people who:

  • Are new to the gym and want to learn safely with someone right there.
  • Feel intimidated by the gym floor and want support to build confidence.
  • Find it hard to stay motivated without someone expecting them.
  • Are working on technical lifts and want live feedback.

Who suits online coaching?

Online coaching tends to suit people who:

  • Already have some gym experience and can follow a program.
  • Have a busy or changing schedule.
  • Live away from the trainer they want to work with.
  • Want ongoing coaching at a lower weekly cost.

Why hybrid coaching is often the sweet spot

Combining the two is often recommended, including by My PT Hub. With a hybrid setup you get:

  • A regular in-person session to coach your technique, check your progress and keep you accountable.
  • An online program for your other days, so every session has a plan.
  • Check-ins and messaging between sessions, so questions do not wait a week.

It costs less than seeing a trainer several times a week, but gives you more support than online coaching alone. Our guide to how often to see a personal trainer explains why one session a week plus a program works for so many people.

Whichever style you choose, if you have an injury or medical condition, check with your GP or physio before starting, and tell your coach so they can plan around it.

What to look for in either option

Whether you choose online coaching, in-person training or a mix, the quality of the coaching matters more than the format. Look for:

  • A personalised program built around your goals, experience and schedule, not a generic template.
  • Regular check-ins so your coach knows how training is going and can adjust it.
  • Technique feedback, either live in the gym or through video form checks.
  • Clear communication, including how and when you can contact your coach.
  • Progression, so the program keeps moving forward as you improve.

A qualified coach who offers these is likely to serve you well in any format. For a checklist, see our guide on how to choose a personal trainer.

How to decide

Ask yourself three questions: how confident am I in the gym, how much hands-on help do I want, and what can I comfortably spend each week? If you are still unsure, book a free consult (details below) and talk it through. The aim is the same either way: to train with purpose and have a clear plan behind every session.

Quick answers

Is online coaching as good as a personal trainer?

It can be for people who are self-motivated and have some gym experience. You lose live, hands-on cueing, but video form checks, check-ins and a personalised program cover a lot of the same ground for less money.

Is online coaching cheaper than personal training?

Usually. Online coaching is typically charged weekly and often costs less than one in-person session, because you train on your own with remote support.

What is hybrid coaching?

Hybrid coaching combines an online program and check-ins with regular in-person sessions. With Jess, Hybrid Coaching is $110 a week and includes one 45-minute session at World Gym Ashmore.

Can beginners do online coaching?

They can, but many beginners benefit from some in-person sessions first to learn technique and equipment set-up. A hybrid approach is a good middle ground.
