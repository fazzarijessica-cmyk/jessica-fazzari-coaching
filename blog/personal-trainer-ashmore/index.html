There's no guesswork about how to get started. The process is the same whether you want in-person sessions, online coaching or a mix:

What your first session involves

A good first personal training session is more of a getting-to-know-you than a test of how hard you can push. According to Nuffield Health, you can usually expect:

  • a chat about your goals, training history and any injuries
  • a health screening form
  • a movement assessment to see how you move, rather than a session designed to wipe you out
  • optional measurements, if they're useful for your goals
  • intensity matched to where you are now

Some muscle soreness in the day or two afterwards is normal. Bring questions, too. There's more on this in what to expect at your first personal training session.

How often should you train?

Australia's physical activity guidelines recommend that adults aged 18–64 do 150–300 minutes of moderate activity or 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity each week, with muscle-strengthening activities on at least two days.

That doesn't mean you need to see a trainer every time you train. A common approach for beginners is two to three sessions a week for around six weeks, then dropping back to one or two (Crunch). Hybrid Coaching follows a similar idea: one coached session a week at Ashmore, with your program guiding the rest of your training on your own.

Signs personal training in Ashmore could help you

You don't need to be a beginner to benefit from a coach, and you don't need to be fit before you start. People most often look for a personal trainer when one of these sounds familiar:

  • You've joined a gym but walk in without a plan and do the same few machines every time.
  • You've been training for a while, but your weights haven't moved in months.
  • You want to learn the main lifts properly, such as squats, deadlifts and presses, without guessing at technique.
  • You start strong every January and drift off by March.
  • You're returning after a break and want a sensible way back in.

A coach's job is to turn that into a clear, progressive program and keep you on it. There's more in what a personal trainer actually does.

In person or online: which suits you?

Living or working near Ashmore makes in-person sessions easy, but they're not the only option.

  • Choose Hybrid if you want hands-on coaching on your technique and equipment set-up each week, plus the structure and check-ins of online coaching.
  • Choose online if your hours change week to week, you already feel comfortable in a gym, or you'd rather keep costs lower.
  • Add a one-off session at $79 if you're coaching online and want someone to look over your lifts in person.

Whichever you choose, the idea behind Jess's coaching is the same: “Train with purpose.” A clear program, regular check-ins and someone in your corner, because intention only gets you so far without direction. See the coaching packages or book a free consult (details below).

If you have an injury, a medical condition, or you haven't exercised in a long time, check with your GP or physio before you start a new training program.
