How long is a personal training session?

Jessica Fazzari Coaching · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

Most personal training sessions are 30, 45 or 60 minutes long. Sixty minutes has long been the traditional default, but 30- and 45-minute sessions are common and can be just as effective when the program is well planned. Your first session is often a little longer because it includes a goals chat and movement assessment.

The common personal training session lengths

Trainers set their own session formats, but you'll usually see three options.

30-minute sessions

Short "express" sessions focus on the essentials: a quick warm-up and the main lifts or a focused circuit. They suit people who are short on time, who already know their way around the gym, or who want more frequent sessions on a budget. According to Bark (January 2026), 30-minute express sessions in Australia typically cost around $40–$70.

45-minute sessions

Forty-five minutes is a practical middle ground. There's enough time for a proper warm-up, a few main exercises with good rest, and some accessory work – without the session dragging. For many people this is long enough to get real work done while still fitting into a lunch break or before work.

60-minute sessions

An hour gives the most room for longer warm-ups, more exercises, extra technique work or conditioning at the end. It can suit people with several goals at once, or those who like a slower pace with more coaching between sets.

What fits into a 45-minute session

Here's an example of how a well-structured 45 minutes might be used. Every trainer and program is different, so treat this as a rough guide.

Part of sessionApprox. timeWhat happens
Check-in2–3 minHow you're feeling, sleep, soreness, any niggles
Warm-up5–8 minMovement prep and lighter sets of the first exercise
Main lifts20–25 minOne or two key exercises (e.g. squat, deadlift variation, press) with technique coaching
Accessory work8–12 minSupporting exercises, often paired to save time
Wrap-up2–3 minNotes on progress and what to do before next session

The key is that the time is planned. A focused 45 minutes with a clear purpose will usually do more for you than an hour of wandering between machines.

Is a longer session better?

Not necessarily. Session length matters less than:

  • Consistency – turning up week after week.
  • Program quality – exercises that suit your goals and progress over time.
  • Effort – working hard enough on your main sets.
  • What you do between sessions – following your program on your own, plus sleep, recovery and nutrition.

If you see a trainer once a week but train two or three more times on your own, a 45-minute session focused on technique and your key lifts can be a very efficient use of your money. If your PT sessions are your only training, a longer session may make more sense.

Why your first session may run longer

First sessions often take more time than regular ones because there's more to cover:

  • Talking through your goals, training history and lifestyle.
  • Completing a health screening form and discussing any injuries.
  • A movement assessment to see how you squat, hinge, push and pull.
  • Optional measurements, if you want them.
  • A gentle introduction to training, rather than a hard workout.

Read more in what to expect at your first personal training session.

If you have an injury or medical condition, mention it before your first session and check with your GP or physio if you're unsure what's safe. Your trainer may adjust session length or intensity to suit.

How to choose the right session length

Ask yourself a few questions:

  1. How much time do you realistically have? A session you can always make is better than a longer one you keep skipping.
  2. How experienced are you? Beginners often benefit from a bit more coaching time; experienced lifters can get a lot from shorter, focused sessions.
  3. Is this your only training? If you'll train on your own as well, shorter PT sessions can focus on the most technical work.
  4. What's your budget? Shorter sessions can let you see a trainer more often for the same spend.

Session length also affects price, which varies by trainer and location. For a full breakdown, see how much a personal trainer costs in Australia.

How to get the most out of your session time

Whatever length you choose, a few habits help you make every minute count:

  • Arrive a few minutes early. Use the time to get changed, fill your water bottle and start moving so the session can begin on time.
  • Tell your trainer how you're feeling. Poor sleep, soreness or a niggle all affect what you should do that day.
  • Eat something beforehand if you need it. Training on an empty tank can make a short session feel much harder.
  • Ask questions. If you're unsure why you're doing an exercise or how it should feel, ask – that's part of what you're paying for.
  • Take notes on your program. Knowing what you lifted last time makes your solo sessions more productive too.

Quick answers

Is a 30-minute personal training session worth it?

It can be, especially if you're short on time or already confident in the gym. A focused 30 minutes on your main lifts can be very productive, though beginners may want a bit more coaching time.

How long should a first personal training session be?

First sessions often run a little longer than usual because they include a goals chat, health screening and movement assessment. Ask your trainer how they structure it.

Is 45 minutes enough for a workout?

Yes, for most people. With a planned program, 45 minutes is enough for a warm-up, main lifts and some accessory work.

Do shorter sessions cost less?

Usually. Bark reported 30-minute express sessions in Australia at around $40–$70 in January 2026, generally less than a full hour.
