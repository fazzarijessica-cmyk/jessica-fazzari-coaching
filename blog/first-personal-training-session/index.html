What to expect at your first personal training session

Jessica Fazzari Coaching · 7 November 2026 · 5 min read

Your first personal training session is mostly about getting to know you. Expect a chat about your goals, training history and any injuries, a health screening form, and a movement assessment where your trainer watches how you move. It should not be a session that leaves you unable to walk the next day; the intensity is matched to where you are.

What happens at your first PT session

The goals and history chat

Most first sessions start with a conversation. Your trainer will want to know:

  • What you want to achieve and why it matters to you.
  • What training you have done before, if any.
  • How many days a week you can realistically train.
  • Your work, sleep and general lifestyle.
  • Any injuries, pain or health conditions.

Be honest here. The more your trainer knows, the better they can plan your program. There are no wrong answers, and "I have never set foot in a gym" is a perfectly good starting point.

The health screening form

You will usually be asked to fill in a pre-exercise health screening form. It asks about things like heart conditions, blood pressure, medications, pregnancy and recent injuries. Nuffield Health lists this as a standard part of preparing for your first session.

It is not there to put you off. It helps your trainer spot anything that needs a medical all-clear before you start.

If you have an injury or a medical condition, it is a good idea to check with your GP or physio before your first session and bring any advice they give you.

The movement assessment

Rather than throwing you straight into a hard workout, a good trainer will watch you move. This might include:

  • Squatting to see your hip, knee and ankle mobility.
  • A hinge or deadlift pattern with light weight or none at all.
  • Pushing and pulling movements such as push-ups or rows.
  • Balance or single-leg exercises.

This shows your trainer where you are starting, what you do well and what needs work. It also means the first exercises in your program fit your body, rather than a generic template.

Optional measurements

Some trainers take measurements in the first session, such as body weight, waist measurements or progress photos. These are usually optional. If you would rather not, say so; strength, energy and how your clothes fit are just as useful ways to track progress. Read more in how long it takes to see results.

The workout: matched to you

Many first sessions include some training, but the intensity should be matched to your fitness and experience. The aim is to learn the movements, get a feel for the right effort level and leave feeling like you can come back, not to prove how hard you can work.

Your trainer will show you how to use the equipment, how each exercise should feel and how to tell when a weight is right for you.

Soreness after your first session

It is normal to feel some muscle soreness a day or two after your first session, especially if you are new to training. It usually eases within a few days and becomes less noticeable as your body gets used to training. Gentle movement such as walking can help.

Sharp pain, or pain that does not settle, is different. Let your trainer know, and see your GP or physio if you are worried.

How to prepare for your first PT session

  • Wear comfortable clothes you can move in, and supportive shoes.
  • Bring water and a small towel.
  • Eat a light meal or snack a couple of hours before, so you are not training on empty or too full.
  • Arrive a few minutes early so you are not rushed.
  • Write down any questions you want to ask.

Questions to ask your trainer

A first session is also your chance to work out whether the trainer is right for you. Good questions include:

  • What will my program look like week to week?
  • What should I do on the days I train on my own?
  • How will we track progress?
  • How do I contact you between sessions?

For more on what to look for, see how to choose a personal trainer and our guide to personal training for beginners.

Feeling nervous? That is normal

Plenty of people feel anxious before their first session, especially if they have not trained before or have had a bad experience at a gym. A good trainer expects this and will not judge where you are starting from. Their job is to meet you at your level and build from there.

A few things that can help settle the nerves:

  • Remember the first session is mostly talking and learning, not testing you.
  • Tell your trainer if you feel unsure or uncomfortable at any point. They can slow down, explain more or change an exercise.
  • Focus on learning one or two things well rather than getting everything perfect.
  • Treat it as a two-way conversation. You are also finding out whether this trainer suits you.

After the session, take a moment to note how you felt and anything you want to ask next time. Most people find the second session already feels far more familiar.

Quick answers

What should I wear to my first personal training session?

Comfortable clothes you can move freely in and supportive shoes. Bring water and a small towel.

Will my first personal training session be hard?

It should not be. A good trainer matches the intensity to your fitness and focuses on learning the movements and assessing how you move.

Is it normal to be sore after my first PT session?

Yes, some muscle soreness for a day or two is normal, especially if you are new to training. Sharp or lasting pain is not, so tell your trainer and see a GP or physio if needed.

Do I have to be weighed at my first session?

Usually not. Measurements are often optional, and strength, energy and how your clothes fit are also good ways to track progress.
